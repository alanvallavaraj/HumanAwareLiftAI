\title{Requirement-aware multi-lift dispatch: restricted waiting and accessibility trade-offs}
\begin{abstract}
Priority service in a lift group shortens waits for passengers who request it and lengthens waits for others, a cost that completed-trip waiting means can hide. This paper represents occupancy, direction compatibility and priority requests as an explicit rule base of dispatch, pickup and boarding rules, so that each passenger requirement can be traced to the rule that acts on it and to the outcome that evaluates it. The rules are evaluated with a restricted mean waiting time over every arriving passenger, reported separately for priority and general requests. In a 16,848-run campaign covering 108 building and demand scenarios, with matched arrival streams and 22,998,183 passenger records, switching rules on one at a time attributes the effects: priority-first boarding alone produces most of the priority benefit and most of the cost to others, while the dispatch bonus and pickup exception add little. With door and transfer delays, the full rule base reduces priority-request waiting from 340.66 to 132.51 model time units relative to an occupancy-matched reference, while general-request waiting rises by 7.26 units (95\% paired scenario-bootstrap interval 5.34 to 9.40) and overall completion does not change detectably. Against collective dispatch, general-request waiting falls instead, so the baseline determines the apparent cost. The pattern holds for three dispatch bonuses and horizons from 300 to 1,200 units. A 279,936-run historical campaign shows the evaluation risk: completed-trip means reported a 5.32\% overall gain for a configuration whose general-request waiting did not improve. The rule base makes priority an explicit, auditable service-allocation decision.
\end{abstract}
